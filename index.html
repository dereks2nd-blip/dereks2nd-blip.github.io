        [ ABOUT ]
        
A bit more context

      

      

        
I'm a CS student, and most of what I actually know came from building things that were a bit beyond me at the time.

        
Cabbage Client is the clearest example — a free, FPS-optimized Minecraft launcher and custom client for Windows. Version management, Microsoft auth, one-click performance mod packs, an in-game HUD with a drag-and-drop editor. Nobody assigned it. I wanted it to exist, and the clients that already did either cost money or weren't good enough.

        
That's mostly how I've picked up a stack: by needing it. TypeScript and Electron because a launcher has to ship as a real desktop app people install. Java because the client has to live inside the game.

        
Right now I'm working toward two things — starting a company, and studying CS at MIT or Caltech. I'm most interested in [ML / Software Engineering], and in the part of engineering where something you made ends up in someone else's hands.

        
Outside of code: [Cooking and Baking].
